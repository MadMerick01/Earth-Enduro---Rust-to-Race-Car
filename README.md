# Earth Enduro

**Rally Stages · CarKov Off-Road**

Independent single-player mod alongside [BeamNG.Earth](https://github.com/TecnaGamer/BeamNG.Earth).
An enduro rally of very long, treacherous stages across the real world. You start each stage in a humble starter car at the start coordinates and try to reach the finish coordinates, earning money as you drive and spending it on repairs and upgrades. It is one save, and you live with your damage and your bills. There is no wreck limit; a car you cannot drive and cannot afford to fix leaves you stuck, and the way out is to start the stage again from the beginning.

The mod (folder, ZIP and code names) is still called `beamng_earth_enduro`, and the starter car file is still `worldironmanenduro_starter.pc`, so existing installs, saves and HUD layouts keep working.

## The rally

**Stages.** The first stage is **Stage 1: Zoji La Pass**, from `34.271288, 75.163671` on the Sind River, Kashmir, to `34.43223, 75.80991` near Dras, Ladakh. The second is **Stage 2: Colo River**, from the Colo River at Colo, NSW (`-33.442415, 150.825953`) to the bush tracks at Colo Heights, NSW (`-33.366081, 150.790919`), with a 30-minute limit. Stages 3 to 8 are six of the world's most treacherous roads: **Death Road**, Bolivia (La Cumbre pass to Yolosa, ~60 km, difficulty 5); **Fairy Meadows Road**, Pakistan (Raikot Bridge to Tato, ~15 km, 5); **Sani Pass**, South Africa to Lesotho (border post to Sani Top, ~9 km, 4); **Skippers Canyon**, New Zealand (Skippers Saddle to Skippers Bridge, ~13 km, 4); **Dalton Highway**, Alaska (Chandalar Shelf over the Atigun Pass to Galbraith Lake, ~61 km, 3); and **Transfăgărășan**, Romania (Cârțișoara to Bâlea Lac, ~30 km, 2). Their coordinates come from Wikipedia, gazetteer and border-post listings and are **untested in game**, and their time limits are estimates. More stages are added by adding one entry to `lua/ge/extensions/beamngEarthEnduro/stages.lua` (an id, a name, start and finish towns, a one-line summary for the Stages tab, a briefing, start and finish coordinates, a finish radius, a time limit, a difficulty from 1 to 5 and the approximate road length in km); the stage list picks it up by itself. The HUD header shows the stage name (on a CarKov run it shows **CarKov Off-Road** and *Reach extraction · 15 km* instead), a briefing when the stage starts, and the finish town and coordinates with a Copy button. **Navigation tip:** paste the finish coordinates into the map search and click the finish point; the map plots a route from where you are and shows arrows and road markers in game. Once you are on the stage the HUD also shows the straight-line distance and compass direction to the finish.

**Starting a stage.** A fresh start (no save yet) or a restarted stage gives you the start coordinates to teleport to, the stage's starting money ($3,000 on Stage 1) and no mileage. When you arrive you are put in the starter car.

**Finishing.** Get within 100 m of the finish coordinates and the HUD celebrates with **STAGE COMPLETE** and pays a **$25,000 stage complete reward**, showing your mileage, money, the reward and sponsor bonuses earned. The finish is saved straight away. The reward is a one-line setting (`FINISH_REWARD`) at the top of `stages.lua`, and a stage can set its own `finishReward`.

**Stage list.** Stages are optional: you never have to finish one to reach another. The Stages tab lists every stage with a one-line summary, its approximate road length and a ✓ once you have finished it; the Route tab only shows the stage you are on. The Stages tab is left out during a CarKov run. **Start** on any stage takes your car, with its parts and any unpaid repair, your money and your held perk there, and gives you its start coordinates to teleport to; mileage, the clock and sponsor bonuses start again. Leaving a stage you have not finished asks you to press **Confirm**, because its mileage and clock are lost. A new player who has not saved yet starts the picked stage fresh, in the starter car with its starting money. Pay for or put back any open parts or car bill first, and quit a CarKov run first. Pay is the same on every stage. After a finish the finish screen still offers **Start** for the stage after it in the list as a shortcut. The save records the stages you have completed, and the car and money you started the stage with.

**CarKov Off-Road.** A side run on its own **CarKov** tab, outside the stages, that you can enter at any time once you are on your stage. **Enter CarKov Off-Road for $2,000** saves your stage where you are, takes the $2,000 fee and picks a random drop point on land anywhere on Earth, plus an extraction point **15 km away in a straight line**. Copy the drop coordinates into the BeamNG.Earth map and teleport there; anywhere within 500 m of the drop point counts, so you can click a little way off on the map to dodge a cliff or river. Your car, with its parts and any repair owed, is waiting. The extraction point needs no road, so it is often an off-road drive: the CarKov tab shows its coordinates and the straight-line distance and compass direction to it. Inside the last kilometre the distance counts down in metres. Get within 100 m of it, with no parts or car bill open, and hold there for 5 seconds while **EXTRACTING...** counts down (leaving the zone starts it again), and you earn **$15,000**. **EXTRACTION COMPLETE** then shows the reward, the run's distance and time, and what the run made after the fee, until you press **Back to my stage**. The run is saved straight away and the HUD sends you back to your stage save, where your car is waiting. Your car and money come with you both ways.

On the run, driving pay ($250/km), repairs, parts, new cars, Save and Service Perks all work as usual (a new perk is offered every 5 km of the run). There is no stage clock, so repairs and new cars cost no time, No Penalty is kept for the stage, and Time Freeze and Pace Gift cannot be activated until you are back. There is no sponsor bonus, and the stage's mileage, clock and sponsor count wait unchanged. **Quit CarKov** (press twice) goes back at any time, keeping the money you earned; the fee is lost. If you are out of money and your car cannot move, the HUD offers **Quit run** instead of Restart stage: the fee is lost, the repair takes as much of your money as it needs, and your car goes back repaired. Insurance pays that repair instead, and the run goes on. While a run is on, the Route tab leaves out the stage (finish and Restart stage) and only says the way out is extraction or quitting. The Garage tab always shows **Buy parts** and **Buy a vehicle** cards when no bill is open, on a run or on a stage, saying where to make the change; the bill then takes their place. The drop and extraction points never land in the sea, a lake, Antarctica, Greenland or the big deserts (the Sahara and its neighbours, Arabia's Rub' al Khali, the Kalahari, the Gobi, the Taklimakan and Australia's Great Sandy, Gibson and Great Victoria deserts), where BeamNG.Earth has little map data. They come from a 0.1° land map built from public-domain Natural Earth data by `tools/make_land_mask.py`; change the areas left out there and run it again. The fee, reward and distance are one-line settings at the top of `carkov.lua`.

**CarKov hostile.** Once per run, somewhere between **3 and 10 km** driven, the message box flashes a red **HOSTILE INBOUND** countdown for 5 seconds, then a hostile car appears 250 m **ahead** of you, 12 m to one side of your line and facing you, and comes at you to ram you, so you can see it coming and take it on. It comes that close because BeamNG.Earth only has solid ground near your car (its terrain block keeps your car at least 250 m from its edge, and more ahead of a moving car, and roads and bridges are solid within 320 m), and a car spawned beyond that falls through the world. It only spawns where a ray finds solid ground, trying closer in (down to 100 m) when there is none, and waits up to 10 seconds for the ground to load if it has to. If it falls through the world anyway (no ground under it for 1.5 seconds, for example when left far behind), it is respawned ahead of you, up to twice a run, then removed. It has a simple brain: more than 120 m away it is steered straight at you by the mod, since BeamNG's chase AI needs roads and Earth often has none; closer in it hands over to the chase AI (and goes back to straight-line driving beyond 180 m). If it is stuck for 4 seconds it reverses out for 2.5 seconds and tries again, up to 3 times in a row (once when already badly damaged) before it gives up and sits still. After it rams you, or when it is pinned against you, it backs off for 1.5 seconds and comes again. Reversing out and backing off never count towards it being disabled. It never comes inside the last kilometre before the extraction point. The hostile counts as **disabled** once it has stood still for 10 seconds *and* has real damage (at least 1,000 damage points), so an AI car that is just stuck pays nothing; then drive within **10 m** of it to earn **$5,000**. Like driving pay, the bounty is kept by **Save**. The hostile is removed when you claim it, when you leave it more than 3 km behind, and when the run ends (extraction or quit) or the level unloads. The hostile drives the **Robber Light**, a black off-road Roamer (4.1 i6, 4WD, 33" tyres) shipped with the mod in `vehicles/roamer/robber_light.pc`; it also appears under Roamer in the vehicle selector. To change it, set `MODEL` and `CONFIG` at the top of `hostile.lua` (both `nil` sends a copy of your own car), where the bounty, distances and timings are one-line settings too. **Untested in game:** the brain's own steering and reversing (the steering direction, the reverse gear shift and the input calls) have not been checked in the game yet; if the hostile steers away from you, set `STEER_SIGN` in `BRAIN` to `-1`. The chase AI follows BeamNG's road network, and how it behaves on BeamNG.Earth's streamed terrain, especially off road, is unproven. It may drive badly or not move at all; a stuck hostile simply never pays out.

**Hostile EMP.** Ported from Bolides The Cut's robber EMP. Once per encounter, after the hostile has rammed you (or has chased you for 30 seconds without landing a hit), getting within **18 m** of it fires an **EMP** at your car: the message box shows **EMP! ENGINE DEAD**, your ignition cuts out, your brakes lock and your hazards come on for **6 seconds**, with a short shove away from the hostile and the EMP sound. It takes **a quarter of your money, at most $5,000**, pulls up for 4 seconds, then flees with it (**ROBBED $X: CATCH IT**), lifting off above about 70 km/h so you can catch it. Disable it as usual to earn the $5,000 bounty **and get the money back**. Because BeamNG.Earth only has ground near your car, a fleeing hostile that gets **250 m** away, or falls through the world, has escaped (**HOSTILE ESCAPED**) and the money is gone. With no money to take it just goes back to ramming you. The stall counts down on game time, so pausing does not use it up, and your car is always given back if the run ends or the hostile is removed mid-EMP. The settings are in `EMP` at the top of `hostile.lua`. **Untested in game:** the EMP's vehicle-side calls (ignition, brake lock, hazards, the thruster shove, the forceField repulsion and the sound) come from Bolides but have not been checked in this mod yet.

**Stage clock.** Each stage has a time limit (2:00:00 for Stage 1). The top of the HUD shows it counting down in h:mm:ss. The clock starts when you reach the start and runs only while the game is not paused, you are in a vehicle with a valid position and the stage is not finished. Each **Repair**, each bought parts bill that includes a repair and each **Buy car** adds a 5-minute penalty; buying parts on an undamaged car costs no time. When the limit runs out the clock turns red and counts up as **OVERTIME**; it is not game over. Next to it, **Pace** shows how many seconds you are ahead (green) or behind (red): the time limit spread evenly over the straight-line distance from start to finish, compared with your time used including penalties. Winding roads can show you behind early and ahead later. Like money, the clock is kept by **Save**, and **Restart stage** resets it.

**Road punctures.** On a stage and on a CarKov run, a tyre now and then bursts from the road: about one every **40 km** at 70 km/h, more often the faster you go (up to 2.5 times as often) and **twice as often off road**. That is roughly a 1 in 3 chance on a 15 km CarKov run. There is never a second puncture within **5 km** of the last one, and never one in the last kilometre before the stage finish or the CarKov extraction. The HUD flashes a red **PUNCTURE!**, and the car really has a flat: the mod calls the car's own `beamstate.deflateRandomTire()`, so it pulls to one side and the tyre flops on the rim. The Garage tab shows the **tyre repair ($300)**, which is added to the repair quote and to any parts or car bill; a repair puts the tyre right. Like other damage, a tyre not repaired before a Save is owed after a reload. Off road means more than 10 m beyond the edge of the nearest road in BeamNG's road map (`map.findClosestRoad`); where the map has no roads to compare with, the car counts as on road. The rates, gap, safe distance and tyre price are one-line settings at the top of `punctures.lua`. **Untested in game:** how complete BeamNG.Earth's road map is, and so how well the off-road test works there, is unproven.

**Roadside events.** On a stage and on a CarKov run, every **20 km** driven there is about a **1 in 3** chance of something happening by the road. The checks fall at 22.5 km, 42.5 km and so on, halfway between perk offers, so an event never comes at the same moment as one, and never in the last kilometre before the stage finish or the CarKov extraction. Half the events are a **friendly village**: a card in the message box reads "A friendly village offers you a free part for your car", naming a real part that fits your car and is better than the one fitted (the cheapest part with a higher game value in the same slot, in a tyre, brake, suspension or engine slot). **Install** fits it, which respawns the car where it stands, so it also comes back **fully repaired, free, with no time penalty, and you keep your perk**; the part counts as paid for, so no parts bill opens. **Decline** turns it down. Install waits while a parts or car bill is open, and an offer is not saved, so a reload turns it down. The other half are **bandits**, who make off with a random **$100 to $1,000** of your money, never taking it below $0. The rates, amounts and slots are one-line settings at the top of `roadEvents.lua`. **Untested in game:** how the mod lists the parts for a slot (`jbeam/io`'s `getAvailableSlotNameMap`, with `getAvailablePartNamesForSlot` as a fallback) and how the game fills the new part's own slots on the respawn are both unproven; with no upgrade found, a village event simply does not happen.

**Out of money and can't drive.** There is no wreck limit and no game over: damage punishes you by itself, with a big repair bill or a car that will not go. One save means you live with your damage and your bills. When your car has at least 1,000 damage points (what BeamNG calls moderate damage), its repair costs more than your balance, and it has not moved more than 5 m in 20 seconds of unpaused play, the HUD shows **OUT OF MONEY AND CAN'T DRIVE?** with the repair and your balance. On a stage it offers **Restart stage**; on a CarKov run it offers **Quit run**. Nothing is forced: drive off, or earn or find the money, and the prompt goes away. If you hold **Insurance**, it pays for the repair instead (costing the usual 5 minutes on a stage). The thresholds are one-line settings at the top of `core.lua`. Saves from older versions that ended in game over are restarted when loaded.

**Restart stage.** Starts the stage again from its start, in the starter car with the starting money on Stage 1, or on a later stage with the car and money you started it with, no mileage and a fresh clock, and replaces your save. The first press asks you to confirm, because your progress is lost.

**New game.** At the bottom of the Tips tab, **New game** starts the whole rally over: Stage 1 in the starter car with $3,000, no finished stages, no perk, no CarKov run, no mileage and no sponsor bonuses. It replaces your save and works on a CarKov run too. The first press asks you to confirm, because none of it can be undone.

These numbers are constants at the top of `core.lua` (`WRECKED_DAMAGE`, `GAME_OVER_DELAY`) and in `stages.lua` (`finishRadiusMetres`, `startingMoney` per stage), so they are easy to tune after testing in game.

This prototype has passed automated logic, filesystem/JSON and HUD lifecycle tests with mocked game APIs.
**Windows BeamNG.drive acceptance is not yet tested.** The mod only needs a valid vehicle position from Earth; it does not wait for Earth's terrain, roads or streaming to finish.

## Requirements

- A licensed Windows BeamNG.drive installation. The mod follows the 0.39.x conventions inspected in Task 001; use 0.39.4 as the initial acceptance target and record the actual game version tested.
- Any BeamNG.Earth version, with its companion server running. The mod is not tied to an Earth version: it reads the vehicle position from the first of these that works — Earth's anchor `enuToGeodetic()` on the live vehicle position, Earth's recorded player position, or `places.here()` — so an Earth update only breaks Enduro if all three change. Every Earth release from `v1.4.5-preview` to `v1.5.5-beta` has been checked with `tools/check_earth_versions.py`.
- No extra Python server or runtime dependencies are required by **the mod** inside the game. Development tests use Python, Lupa and Node.js.

## Install on Windows

1. [Download the latest `beamng_earth_enduro.zip`](https://github.com/MadMerick01/BeamNG-Earth-Enduro/releases/latest/download/beamng_earth_enduro.zip). Older builds are listed on the [Releases page](https://github.com/MadMerick01/BeamNG-Earth-Enduro/releases).
2. Copy that single ZIP into your active **BeamNG user folder's `mods` directory**. Use the game launcher's user-folder tools to locate it; do not put the ZIP in Steam's game binaries. **Do not extract it.** Remove/disable an older copy of this mod to avoid duplicate extensions; keep your user settings and save files.
3. With the supported Earth mod and its companion running separately, enable the mod, load Earth Freeroam, and use **UI Apps → Add App → Earth Enduro**. Move/resize it as desired. The mod never replaces the layout.

   The HUD keeps the stage, the stage clock and pace, status, any STAGE COMPLETE or OUT OF MONEY AND CAN'T DRIVE banner, your money and **Save** always in view, and puts everything else on four tabs: **Route** (briefing, start or resume and finish coordinates with Copy buttons, finish distance, the navigation tip, Restart stage and the stage list), **Garage** (parts and car bills and repairs), **CarKov** (entering a CarKov run, and its drop and extraction points while you are on one) and **Tips** (the briefing, tips and perks). A dot marks a tab with something waiting, such as an open bill or repair owed, and a new parts or car bill or coordinates to go to open their tab.
4. The HUD shows the start coordinates, the Stage 1 start on the Sind River, Kashmir **`34.271288, 75.163671`** for a new profile. Copy them, paste them into the BeamNG.Earth map and teleport there. Once you arrive, the HUD says you are in the right place and Save is available.

No JSON edits, extraction, Python, Node.js or command-line commands are needed to install or play the rally. The ZIP has `scripts/`, `lua/` and `ui/` directly at its root. The mod remains independent of Earth. ZIPs are published as GitHub Releases, not committed to the implementation source tree.

## Default base and optional advanced configuration

The built-in base (where a new profile starts) is the first stage's start, on the Sind River, Kashmir: **latitude `34.271288`, longitude `75.163671`**. It is used automatically when no user configuration exists. A `base` in the configuration moves the first stage's start, which is mainly useful for testing; the finish stays where the stage puts it. No settings files are created on load, and no existing configuration or saved profile is overwritten.

Optional overrides remain available at `<user folder>/settings/beamngEarthEnduro/config.json` (VFS `/settings/beamngEarthEnduro/config.json`). Only users who want an override need to copy/edit the included `config.example.json`:

```json
{
  "base": { "latitude": 34.271288, "longitude": 75.163671 },
  "resumeRadiusMetres": 50
}
```

Latitude must be -90..90 and longitude -180..180. `resumeRadiusMetres` (1..1000, default 50) is how close the vehicle must get to the start or last save coordinates to count as arrived. A legacy `stabilitySeconds` value is ignored. A present but invalid/unreadable override blocks activation and saving rather than being silently replaced by defaults. The rally reloads by itself when the file changes, or load the level again.

An existing profile retains its original base even when the configuration changes. For a new profile, the base becomes fixed when the session first gets a valid vehicle position, including across configuration reloads in that running instance. An unsaved profile does not survive exiting the game. Back up existing profile files before intentionally starting a different profile; there is no reset/delete button in this prototype.

## Start, save and resume

1. Load Earth Freeroam and enter an active vehicle. The HUD shows one set of coordinates: your last save, or the start (the base) if you have not saved yet. Press **Copy**, paste them into the BeamNG.Earth map and teleport there; The mod never submits the teleport itself. When the vehicle is within `resumeRadiusMetres` (50 m) of them, the HUD says *You're in the right place* and you carry on from there. Until then, Save is locked and driving earns no mileage or money, so a Save cannot overwrite your trek with wherever the game happened to spawn you.

   **Starter car:** on a fresh start (no save yet, or both save files unreadable) or after **Restart stage** the mod swaps your vehicle, when you arrive at the start, for the **Earth Enduro Starter**, a 1950s Burnside coupe with a stock inline-six. Its parts count as paid for; upgrade it from there with **Parts** and keep it running with **Repair**. The config also appears under Burnside in the game's vehicle selector.

   **Your car is saved:** **Save** also keeps your car: its paid-for parts (an open parts bill is left out), paint and tuning. Each time a save loads (game start or level load) your vehicle is swapped for that car when you arrive at the last save coordinates, so you carry on in the car you saved. Like money, part changes made since the last Save are undone by a reload; before the first Save, a reload puts you back in the starter car. Saves made before this version have no car stored, and Enduro leaves your vehicle alone until your next Save.

   **Damage is owed, not forgiven:** Save also records how damaged the car is. The restored car comes back looking undamaged, but that repair is still owed: the HUD shows *Repair still owed*, every repair quote and parts bill includes it, and it is carried through later saves until you pay it with **Repair** or a parts bill. Restarting the game therefore never gives a free repair. Dents are not put back on the restored car.
2. Drive anywhere. Saving is not locked to the spot you arrived at. Click **Save** to keep the vehicle's current valid geographic position. No periodic or shutdown autosave occurs.

   While READY, the HUD's **Mileage** counts the distance your vehicle covers between readings. Jumps faster than 150 m/s (540 km/h), such as teleports, are not counted, and nothing is counted while the vehicle position is unavailable. Mileage driven since the last Save is shown as *unsaved*; it is stored by **Save** and lost if you exit or load the level again without saving.

   The HUD's **Money** goes up by **$250 for every kilometre** counted as mileage, so teleports earn nothing. The balance is whole dollars; part-dollars carry over while you keep driving. For testing, the **+$10000 (test)** button adds $10,000 straight away. Like mileage, money earned since the last Save is shown as *unsaved*, kept by **Save** and lost on exit or when the level loads again without saving.

   **Sponsor bonus:** every **10 km** of counted mileage also pays a **$4,000 sponsor bonus**, and teleports never count toward it. A bar under the money shows how far it is to the next one ("Sponsor bonus in 3.2 km"), and the message box flashes a gold **+$4,000 SPONSOR BONUS** when it pays. The number of bonuses paid is saved with the balance, so a bonus is never paid twice, and unsaved bonuses go with a reload like any other money. **Restart stage** resets it, and the finish screen shows how many you earned. Bonuses stop once the stage is finished. A stage can set its own `sponsorBonus` and `sponsorEveryKm` in `stages.lua`; the defaults are one-line settings at the top of that file.
   **Service Perks:** you hold one perk at a time, shown as a purple badge next to your money. Every **5 km** of counted mileage (teleports never count) a random perk is offered in the message box: **Swap** takes it in place of yours, **Keep** turns it down. Any repair loses the perk you hold, except Insurance. Passive perks work by themselves the next time they apply: **Double Cash** (driving pays $500/km while held), **Sponsor Boost** (next sponsor bonus pays double), **No Penalty** (next repair or new car costs no time; a repair uses it instead of losing it), **Parts Discount** (half off the parts on the next parts bill), **Car Dealer Deal** (next new car 30% off) and **Insurance** (pays for the repair once when you are out of money and your damaged car cannot move). Three need **Activate**: **Time Freeze** (the clock stops for 3 minutes of driving), **Pace Gift** (2 minutes off the clock) and **Lucky Mile** (the next 5 km pay triple, $750/km). An activated perk leaves the slot and keeps running, even through a repair. The perk, the offer and anything running are saved with your progress. **Restart stage** goes back to the perk you started the stage with, and **Start Stage 2** takes your held perk with you (a running perk or an open offer stays behind). All perk values are one-line settings at the top of `perks.lua`.

   **Repair:** press **Get repair quote** to price the damage to the vehicle you are in. The price comes from the vehicle's overall damage and what the car is worth (its game Value, or $10,000 when it has none): a $100 call-out plus $0.50 per damage point on a $25,000 car, scaled up or down with the car's Value, so a $5,000 car pays $0.10 a point and a $100,000 car $2. Nothing under 50 points (a scratch) is charged, and the damage part never costs more than 75% of the car's Value. A punctured tyre adds damage priced the same way ($300 on a $25,000 car). Press **Repair** to pay it; the vehicle is respawned where it stands with its current parts, tuning and paint, so every part is new. If you cannot afford it, nothing is charged or repaired and the HUD says how much more you need. If the damage changes or you switch vehicle after the quote, Repair re-quotes instead of charging. Money spent is shown as *spent, unsaved* until you **Save**.

   **Parts:** change parts in BeamNG's own parts menu as usual. The parts the vehicle had when you got in count as paid for. After each change the HUD's **Parts** section lists the parts added and taken off and the bill. A part costs its value from the game's part files, or $250 if it has none; parts taken off in the same change are traded in for 30% of their value, which lowers the bill but never pays out. Press **Buy** to pay and keep the parts (it is disabled when you cannot afford it), or **Revert** to put the paid parts back; tuning and paint are free and kept either way. Trying parts on and swapping back costs nothing. If you drive more than 25 m without paying, the paid parts are put back automatically. A part change respawns the vehicle and mends all damage, so when the vehicle was damaged the repair price is added to the bill. Revert or driving off charges nothing: the car comes back with the paid parts looking new, but its repair is still owed, as after a reload. Repair is locked while a parts bill is open. Repair is also locked while a car bill is open.

   **Cars:** pick a car in BeamNG's own vehicle selector, or get into any car that is not yours, and the HUD's **Car** section shows a bill: the car's price from the game (the Value in its config's info file, or $10,000 if it has none), plus any parts you change on it, less the trade-in of your car. The trade-in is half your car's Value less the repair it needs, and never below $0, so a cheaper car can cost nothing but never pays out. Press **Buy car** to pay and keep it, or **Revert** to get your car back; driving more than 25 m without paying does the same. If the new car replaced yours, yours is respawned where you stand looking new, but its repair is still owed. If it was spawned as an extra car, you are put back in your own car, damage and all, and the extra car is left parked. While a car bill is open, your old car's damage still counts towards being out of money and unable to drive, and Save keeps your old car. The starter car and the saved car you get on arrival are yours and never billed; the starter car's Value is $3,500.
3. Restart the game. The HUD shows your last saved coordinates and your distance from them; go there as in step 1 to continue.

The Save button reads the vehicle position again at click time and saves that. It cannot save with no vehicle or invalid coordinates. Vehicle model and the paid-for car config are saved when available. Altitude and heading are omitted because a reliable native retrieval contract for those values has not been established for this task. Loading a save does not move you to the saved location; the saved car is respawned where you stand once you arrive.

If the vehicle position is temporarily unavailable (no vehicle, Earth not running), the HUD shows EARTH UNAVAILABLE and returns to READY wherever the vehicle is as soon as a position is available again; once you have arrived, you do not have to go back. This never changes the saved location or base. A mission start or extension reload reloads the save and discards unsaved mileage and money.

## Save files and recovery

The mod stores schema-1 profile `default` in `/settings/beamngEarthEnduro/save-a.json` and `save-b.json`. Each Save writes the file that does not hold your newest save, then reads it back to check it, so a failed save never damages the last good one. On load, the newest readable file is used. New profiles exist in memory until the first manual Save.

- **Save failed:** the HUD says *Could not save just now. Your last save is still safe. Press Save to try again.* Nothing is locked; keep playing and press Save again.
- **One file damaged:** the other one is loaded, with no action needed. The next Save replaces the damaged file.
- **Both files unreadable:** you get a fresh start, and the HUD says so. Saving replaces the unreadable files, so if you want them back, copy them somewhere else before you press Save.
- **RALLY PAUSED:** only for a save made by a newer version (update the mod), a save folder the game cannot open, or an internal problem. The HUD says which. Loading the level again loads your last save; anything since then is lost.

Two generations are recoverable storage, not a guarantee against power loss, external simultaneous edits, disk failure or malicious tampering. Save files contain geographic locations; treat them as your user data. Uninstalling the mod does not require deleting settings/saves.

## Development and packaging

From the repository root, with Python 3.10+ and Node.js installed:

```sh
python -m venv .venv
# Windows PowerShell:
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m pip install -r tests/requirements.txt
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tests/run.py
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tools/package.py
```

On Linux/macOS use `.venv/bin/python` in place of `.venv\Scripts\python.exe`. Lupa is pinned and supplies LuaJIT 2.1 for tests; an installed native `luajit` can run the Lua suites instead, but the Python bridge tests still require Lupa. Python/Lupa/Node are not shipped in the mod.

The archive is generated at `dist/beamng_earth_enduro.zip`. Packaging is deterministic and includes installation, architecture and acceptance documentation; it does not package user settings, saves or development dependencies. Every push to `main` runs the tests, builds the ZIP and publishes it as a new release tagged `build-<number>` (see `.github/workflows/release.yml`); if the tests fail, no release is made. The Python/Node commands above are for developers rebuilding/testing the mod, not for players installing the supplied ZIP. Developers can alternatively copy `mod/beamng_earth_enduro/` into the user's `mods/unpacked/beamng_earth_enduro/`; do not install both copies.

After a BeamNG.Earth update, check that the mod can still read positions from it (reads a local git clone of Earth; changes nothing):

```sh
git clone https://github.com/TecnaGamer/BeamNG.Earth.git
.venv\Scripts\python.exe tools/check_earth_versions.py BeamNG.Earth
```
